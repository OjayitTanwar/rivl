## Outcome and current boundary

Automatically contribute eligible evidence from newly recorded activities while the contribution setting is enabled, and let athletes discover shared infrastructure with its evidence, access limitations and freshness visible. The user has selected default-on contribution; new eligible device recordings and Watch imports now queue automatically in source. This is not a history backfill, and no hosted sharing is configured. Include cycling, running, walking and hiking. GPS can support a path's observed use; it cannot establish physical protection, lawful access, accessibility, condition or safety. Repeated GPS agreement does not remove that limitation. Unknown remains a valid result.

Existing building blocks:

- `src/services/path-network.ts` queries OpenStreetMap/Overpass near a selected point. Current bounds are 1.2 km, 500 ways, 20,000 vertices, a 10-second request interval and bounded response size. It retains backwards-compatible geometry arrays plus typed OSM features (way identity, node IDs when complete, bounded tags, mapped access, direction, layer and bridge/tunnel metadata). Its classifier distinguishes painted/shared lanes from dedicated cycleways without inferring physical protection or local legal permission. The legacy offline graph still lacks node-aware directional routing.
- `src/domain/offline-routing.ts` routes over retained coordinates, uses a 20 m endpoint proximity limit, excludes gaps longer than 250 m and declines disconnected/out-of-area requests. It currently connects equal coordinates and adds both directions. It lacks OSM node identity, layer/bridge/tunnel and directional-edge semantics; it must not become the community graph unchanged.
- `src/services/offline-maps.ts` saves at most five encrypted local geometry areas and instructions. Saved geometry is not a full offline basemap. Community snapshots need a separate schema, expiry and revocation mechanism.
- `src/domain/geo.ts`, `supabase/migrations/202610080001_sync_privacy.sql` and `published_activities` provide endpoint/privacy-zone redaction, including sparse edges crossing a zone. The activity social projection contains activity identity/summary and is not a suitable anonymous contribution projection.
- `supabase/migrations/202610100002_popularity.sql` counts distinct owners on coarse cells, currently with a three-owner minimum. Popularity is not evidence of a protected track; this SQL is not a contribution consent pipeline.
- `supabase/functions/process-segments`, its shared port and `202610100001_segment_processing.sql` demonstrate bounded jobs, leases, revision checks and transactional completion. Segment participation is independent of track contribution and must remain so. Existing hosted worker/scheduler operation is still unverified in the handoff.
- Maps, planner, navigation, saved routes, privacy settings and account deletion provide integration points. Existing path-network, route, privacy, segment and deletion tests provide patterns, not proof of the new system.

Do not invent data, automatically submit edits to OSM, introduce paid inference, or claim worldwide completeness. Keep the core mapping/planning priorities in HANDOFF ahead of rollout.

## Domain contract and honest claims

`src/domain/community-tracks.ts` now provides source decoders, versioned evidence policy and bounded fragment preparation. Its prepared fragments stay internal and ineligible for direct publication. Recording and Watch lifecycle now invoke it; the server derives only coarse cells after corroboration. Build the remaining topology-aware track graph on this foundation rather than extending `Activity` with public mapping fields. Geometry must be a list of independent line fragments; never flatten a privacy gap into a connecting line.

Every candidate has: stable ID; geometry version; status; source kind and license; permitted public geometry; segment-level attributes; evidence categories; freshness; review date; restrictions/flags; and revision. Each attribute stores its provenance and observation date, including explicit `unknown` when no evidence supports it. Geometry confidence, physical separation, sport suitability and access are independent dimensions.

Infrastructure kinds: `protected_cycle_track`, `painted_cycle_lane`, `shared_cycle_lane`, `shared_path`, `pedestrian_path`, `trail`, `running_track`, `ordinary_road`, `unknown`. Physical separation: `observed_barrier`, `mapped_separation`, `paint_only`, `shared`, `unknown`. Pedestrian/cyclist segregation is a distinct attribute. OSM `segregated=yes` describes separation between path users; it can include a painted dividing line and does not prove protection from motor vehicles. [OSM segregated documentation](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:segregated).

`highway=cycleway` describes a mapped cycling way. Retain its additional tags and provenance; do not infer the entire route is protected, current, or accessible to every sport. [OSM cycleway documentation](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag:highway%3Dcycleway).

For each sport, store suitability as `reported_suitable`, `reported_unsuitable` or `unknown`; separately store access as `mapped_allowed`, `mapped_restricted`, `conditional`, `reported_restriction`, or `unknown`. Running and walking may share pedestrian access rules but their suitability differs; hiking suitability needs terrain/grade evidence, not merely the presence of a path. Cycling suitability must not imply foot access. `designated` identifies a designated mode, and other access restrictions require their own tags/evidence. [OSM designated access documentation](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag:access%3Ddesignated).

Preserve original way/node IDs, source revision, `oneway`, `oneway:bicycle`, directional cycleway tags, bridge, tunnel, layer, crossing/barrier and conditional restrictions. Equal screen coordinates or intersecting lines never establish graph connectivity. A bridge above a road is not a junction; a nearby painted bike lane is not a protected parallel track. Unknown direction/access/topology excludes a candidate from routing when the proposed movement cannot be justified.

Evidence categories are explainable facts, such as mapped tags, several eligible independent observations, user description, reviewed photo, and reviewer decision. Do not display fabricated percentage confidence. Numeric model probabilities require labeled calibration data and validation before any product use.

Statuses and transitions:

1. `proposed`: contributor draft, private to owner; no public geometry.
2. `unverified`: accepted evidence awaiting enough support/review; visible only to owner/moderators in the initial release.
3. `corroborated`: eligible independent evidence meets the configured threshold; this supports observed use, not protection or legal access.
4. `reviewed`: authorized moderator reviewed specific attributes with dated evidence and a reason. This replaces vague “approved infrastructure” language; it is not safety certification.
5. `disputed`: unresolved material conflict, restriction, privacy or abuse report. Remove affected routing eligibility immediately; a serious privacy report suppresses geometry pending review.
6. `withdrawn`: revoked, deleted, obsolete or rejected; no public geometry. Keep only minimal necessary tombstone/audit metadata under a documented retention policy.

A moderator can return a candidate to an earlier state with a reason. Flags include uncertain separation, possible road match, access unknown, seasonal/conditional closure, stale data, poor location quality, source conflict, suspected spoofing, privacy concern and unsafe-content report. Flags must not be concealed by a favorable status.

## Automatic contribution setting and public geometry

Automatic contribution is ON by default, as requested by the user. Add a clearly disclosed setting and simple off switch; do not require per-workout approval, public activity sharing or manual fragment selection. Once rollout capability is available and required platform location permissions are granted, newly recorded eligible running, cycling, walking and hiking activities automatically enqueue evidence when the setting is on. The setting is independent of activity visibility, heatmaps, segment participation, live safety sharing and automatic workout detection. A private activity remains private while eligible derived infrastructure evidence can contribute to the shared map. Turning off stops future collection/submission and cancels pending uncommitted work; a separate “Delete my previous contributions” action retracts prior evidence. Never hide that distinction.

Open-source application code and publication of personal GPS records are separate choices. Automatic contribution publishes eligible derived candidate information under the rules below, not personal activity identities or raw endpoint traces. Do not silently sweep historical workouts; the default covers new recordings only. Imported files, friends' workouts, manual/fabricated tracks, demos and simulations are excluded from automatic GPS support. Any historical contribution option is a separate future product decision.

Provide a contribution history/detail preview for inspection and correction, without making it an approval gate. Automatic GPS submission makes only claims supported by validated observations; it cannot automatically assert “protected”, “safe” or lawful access. User descriptions or optional photos are separate deliberate evidence actions. Raw sensor data stays in encrypted local storage by default. Do not create another server raw-trace collection. Existing cloud tracks may be processed only through restricted owner/service access while the automatic contribution preference is enabled; this is not end-to-end encryption and must not be described as such. New retained private evidence, if necessary, needs a documented encryption/key-management design and retention policy before upload is enabled.

Apply at least the existing 200 m start/finish exclusion and all configured privacy zones on-device and again in the trusted server pipeline. Cut complete crossing edges and keep fragments disconnected. Reapply privacy on source revision, zone changes and preference changes. Detect short activities that leave no publishable fragment and explain that nothing can be contributed. Avoid removing endpoint radius after map matching: matching first can move a point outside its exclusion.

Public candidate geometry is a reviewed/aggregated infrastructure representation, not an individual trace, timestamp series, exact observed entry/exit location or contributor-linked polyline. Prefer licensed existing OSM way geometry for an established path. An unmapped corridor may be assembled only after privacy review and enough independent eligible support; retain a coarse public corridor until a reviewer can establish legitimate infrastructure geometry. Quantization alone is insufficient protection. Never expose trace timestamps, speed, HR, power, activity IDs, home zones, contributor IDs or exact observation times.

Proposed pilot publication minimum: five eligible distinct eligible contributors with contribution enabled, observed on at least three different days, counting one contribution per owner/candidate/time window. These are conservative starting controls, not calibrated privacy guarantees or proof of five different people. Tune against sparse-area/reidentification and false-corroboration fixtures. No exact contributor counts, fine temporal breakdowns or filter combinations that reveal a person. Use a broad “several independent contributions” label. If revocation drops support below the minimum, remove the derived public geometry until recomputed. An OSM-only baseline may remain with its OSM provenance, independent of withdrawn community evidence.

Prevent public bbox/time/sport queries from reconstructing private traces through differencing: minimum fixed spatial query size, stable response buckets, aggregate output only, bounded rate and query size, no per-contribution feed. Do not ship contributor attribution or public leaderboards for mapping in the first version.

## Database and RPC design

Create new migrations after the current repository's last timestamp; confirm ordering before naming them. Split schema/RLS, mutation/job RPCs, public read projection and deletion integration into reviewable changes.

- `track_mapping_settings`: owner primary key, enabled, disclosure version, monotonically increasing preference revision, enabled/disabled timestamps. Owner reads their row; changes go through validated RPCs.
- `track_contributions`: ID, owner, source activity reference/revision (nullable for a manual observation), preference revision, idempotency key, private selected/redacted fragments or restricted evidence reference, claims, state, algorithm version, retention expiry, created/updated timestamps. Unique owner/idempotency key. Owner reads their own safe metadata; no client writes to server-owned processing fields. Contributors do not read each other's evidence.
- `track_candidates`: internal versioned geometry, source/license provenance, typed attributes, internal review state and freshness. Moderator/service access only; no anonymous table read.
- `track_candidate_evidence`: candidate/contribution links, private owner identity for distinct counting, eligibility/exclusion reason, source/privacy/preference revision, evidence category and algorithm version. Unique candidate/contribution/version tuple; never publicly readable.
- `track_mapping_jobs`: contribution or candidate key, expected input revisions, state, lease token/expiry, attempts, retry time, last safe error code. Service-only, with indexes for pending jobs and bounded footprint queries.
- `published_track_candidates`: sanitized geometry and attributes, public revision, source/attribution, public status, freshness/expiry, reason flags and source license. Allow authenticated bounded spatial RPC reads initially; expose no private joins. Public discovery does not require contribution to be enabled.
- `track_mapping_reports`, `track_mapping_review_events`: bounded reports and append-only moderator decisions. Reporter reads own receipt; reviewers use restricted access. Public reasons are sanitized and contain no identifiers/private descriptions.
- `track_mapping_tombstones`: candidate/revision withdrawal markers and regional generation numbers for invalidation. No raw evidence or contributor identity.

Enable RLS on every table, explicitly revoke blanket anon/authenticated privileges, and deny direct client inserts/updates/deletes that bypass quotas or projection maintenance. RPCs use fixed search paths, bounded parameters and explicit `auth.uid()` ownership checks. A worker role/secret cannot be used by clients; moderator authority is server-managed, not a profile flag a user can edit.

Initialize new feature preferences to enabled on authenticated feature initialization, with the rollout/disclosure version and a server timestamp; preserve an existing disabled choice on reinstall, account sync and upgrades. A missing local setting during offline first use may use the disclosed default for encrypted local drafts, but remote submission waits for authenticated initialization and its returned revision. Do not fabricate an affirmative user-click timestamp. Add `delete_my_track_contributions` as a distinct idempotent RPC; disabling the preference cancels pending submissions/jobs but leaves previously committed evidence until deletion, source deletion or expiry. This separation must appear in settings and tests.

RPC contracts: `set_track_mapping_preference`, `submit_track_contribution`, `withdraw_track_contribution`, `read_my_track_contributions`, `report_track_candidate`, `review_track_candidate`, `list_track_candidates`, `track_candidate_changes`; and service-only `claim_track_mapping_job`, `track_mapping_job_input`, `complete_track_mapping_job`, `retry_track_mapping_job`. Submission atomically checks authenticated owner, source ownership, current enabled preference, quotas, input version/bounds and idempotency; the same key plus same payload returns the existing result, while reused key with conflicting content fails. Never trust client candidate status, independent-owner count or reviewed claims.

Cap initial contribution payload at 1 MB, 5,000 vertices and 20 selected fragments; reject rather than silently truncate. Validate finite coordinates, timestamp monotonicity for private evidence, encoding version, source time bounds and fragment integrity. Photo rollout is separately gated below. Spatial reads use a maximum 2 km radius, 200 candidates and 20,000 public vertices with stable pagination and an explicit incomplete-coverage result.

## Processing, matching and lifecycle

Implement `supabase/functions/_shared/track-mapping.ts` as a pure port-based processor and `process-track-mapping/index.ts` as the small trusted adapter. Reuse segment-worker lease/revision concepts, not its public-activity eligibility rules. Each invocation handles one leased job, with a hard deadline; scheduler operation and authentication require real hosted verification.

Processing order: claim → verify lease → retrieve current preference/source/privacy revisions → validate evidence → redact → quality filter → bounded source-network lookup from licensed retained data → identify alternatives → produce evidence/candidate proposal → transactional completion. Do not call Google directions/places/tiles to derive a persistent community dataset. If matching is ambiguous, save an unresolved private proposal or reject with an understandable reason.

GPS filters must preserve gaps, pauses and accuracy evidence, exclude demos/simulations/manual fabricated tracks from independent GPS support, reject teleporting/nonmonotonic points, impossible sport speeds and long unobserved gaps, and avoid connecting imported discontinuities. Conservative pilot quality proposal: accuracy no worse than 20 m, gaps no longer than 30 seconds within a matched observation, and at least 100 m contiguous eligible support; these require field calibration. Low-quality evidence never becomes a precise public line through interpolation. Manual observations can be reviewed but do not count as GPS corroboration.

Map matching compares plausible alternatives and their topology/direction, not nearest-polyline distance alone. If reported accuracy overlaps both roadway and adjacent track, physical separation remains unknown. Urban canyon, tree cover, parallel paths, opposing carriageways, tunnels and bridges are required ambiguity cases. Run/walk/hike choice does not prove facility classification. Absence from OSM does not prove absence on the ground; OSM disagreement requires review, not silent overriding.

Mitigate spoofing/Sybil support with deduplication, bounded account/day submissions, one owner vote per candidate window, suspicious repeated-identical geometry detection, account-risk quarantine and moderator sampling. No device attestation or account-age heuristic proves distinct humans. Do not collect intrusive identifiers merely to manufacture confidence. Proposed pilot quota is 10 submissions/account/day and at most 100 queued jobs globally; exceeding it returns a retryable explanation without enabling publication.

Completion takes database locks on the job, contribution and relevant candidate; compares lease, source revision, preference revision, privacy revision, candidate geometry revision and algorithm version; rechecks eligibility inside the transaction; replaces evidence idempotently; recalculates support; writes the sanitized projection/tombstone and regional version together. Stale leases or changed preference cannot publish results. Concurrent contributors must not lose support; candidate merge/split operations transfer only eligible evidence and bump geometry versions.

Deleting all previous contributions or withdrawing one contribution immediately disables its evidence in the same mutation transaction and suppresses any projection that may depend on it pending recomputation. Source deletion, privacy-zone changes, moderation, source revisions and algorithm corrections follow the same invalidation path. No background worker may restore withdrawn data. Account deletion includes new tables, private media keys, reports as appropriate, evidence links, pending jobs and derived support; integrate with existing retryable deletion processing. Distinguish withdrawal of one contribution from deletion of the whole candidate when other independent/licensed evidence remains.

Private evidence retention proposal: delete processing input within seven days after completion, retain only minimal eligibility metadata for 90 days, and retain candidate-linked support only until the contribution is withdrawn or deleted. Product/legal review must settle retention and whether evidence must be revalidated after expiry. A candidate cannot claim current community corroboration after all retained valid support expires.

## App, offline and moderation integration

Add `src/services/community-tracks.ts` with bounded typed decoders and owner-scoped contribution outbox; idempotency survives restarts and retries. A disabled preference never silently resubmits a queued contribution; every submission and completion rechecks its version. Sign-out keeps owners isolated; account deletion clears the local queue. Add contribution history/detail and optional manual-evidence screens using the current design system and accessibility conventions; settings contains the default-on preference, history and deletion controls. Do not preselect public sharing to make contribution possible.

Hook automatic draft creation after successful real activity persistence in `src/services/recorder-engine.ts` (`save`), using the saved activity/source revision as the idempotency input. Failure to enqueue community evidence must never fail or erase the athlete's saved workout; persist a retryable mapping task separately. Do not hook generic imports, social reads or every activity database write. Recovered real recordings can contribute once after successful save, while explicit simulation flags and provenance checks exclude synthetic recordings. For local-only accounts, keep eligible drafts encrypted locally and explain that shared-map submission needs connectivity and an authenticated owner; never invent global support from local drafts.

Candidate details show observed infrastructure type, supported sports, access separately, evidence labels, source attribution, freshness, limitations and Report. Planning may incorporate a candidate only after review, licensed graph connectivity, known directional/access compatibility and fresh restrictions; discovery can precede routing integration.

Upgrade `PathNetwork` without breaking legacy display consumers: retain `lines`, add typed ways/edges and source metadata, and explicitly mark legacy geometry as topology unknown. Extend graph and offline fixtures before enabling routing over these ways. Never add a connector across privacy holes, unverified crossings or neighboring disconnected paths. Restricted/conditional access takes precedence over popularity. Existing Google/Apple base maps remain display providers; community provenance is shown in plain language, without claiming a provider endorsed the path.

Community offline snapshots are encrypted, owner-scoped, bounded, versioned and separate from existing path areas. Proposed maximum five regions/10 MB total, with 24-hour eligibility expiry and seven-day maximum display retention. Query tombstones/region generation before using a connected cache; withdraw immediately on received invalidation. A disconnected device cannot learn immediate revocation: show downloaded age, expire its community layer automatically and never promise instantaneous worldwide deletion of offline copies. No stale candidate can regain reviewed routing eligibility without refresh. App upgrades migrate or discard incompatible versions. Basemap tile packs remain out of scope.

Initial moderation is a restricted authenticated review screen backed by audited RPCs, with a named operating owner and response expectations before launch. Include duplicate merges, conflicting claims, landowner access reports, closures, private-property concerns, harassment/spam, review appeals and emergency suppression. Do not invite users to trespass or investigate hazardous sites. Reports remain private; queue bounds and rate limits cannot block a privacy withdrawal.

Optional photos should launch after geometry privacy works: explicit photo consent and rights confirmation; strip EXIF including GPS/date/device metadata; downscale/re-encode; enforce MIME/signature/size limits; private bucket with short-lived reviewer URLs; no public originals. Reject or redact identifiable faces/license plates/private residences before public use. Do not log signed URLs or sensitive photo descriptions. A reporter can request photo removal independently; delete media through retryable jobs, with immediate access revocation. In version one, text/manual observation is sufficient and photo upload may remain disabled.

## Budget and sources

Use the existing optional Supabase stack and bounded deterministic processing. “Zero additional paid services” is a product constraint, not a promise of unlimited free hosted capacity. Keep contribution discovery local and demand-driven; no global crawling, continuous GPS uploads, unlimited Overpass retries, automatic photo/AI analysis or paid geocoder dependency. Cache licensed network metadata with explicit provider policy; back off on busy responses and return unavailable honestly.

Before hosted enablement, measure the actual project's free capacity and configure server-side daily job, storage, request and egress ceilings below it. Proposed pilot ceiling: 100 processed jobs/day, one concurrent worker, 100 pending jobs and 100 MB retained private evidence. These values are starting product limits, not claims about provider quotas. The budget guard applies before enqueue/external fetch; deletion and revocation retain reserved capacity. On budget exhaustion, keep private drafts, stop collection that cannot be processed, and show deferred/unavailable state. Add a kill switch independent of client release. No scheduler deployment or billing changes occur under this plan alone.

Retain OSM attribution and source/license metadata through export and offline display. OSM is ODbL data with attribution obligations and potential database share-alike requirements; assess the derived/collective database boundary before distributing an aggregated OSM-derived database. Separate original user observations from licensed source geometry in storage. [OpenStreetMap copyright and licensing](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright). Do not remove attribution by calling a derived track “community data.”

Google-derived routes, Places results, tiles, screenshots or labels must not seed this persistent community database. This is a conservative implementation boundary; any future provider use requires its then-current contract and allowed retention to be checked explicitly. No automated OSM edits. A future human export flow requires separate source/license and editing-policy review.

Phase 3 — bounded worker and review: leased pipeline, deterministic eligibility, thresholds, conservative matching, review/report RPCs and restricted moderator UI. Gate: idempotent retries, stale-worker rejection, candidate merge/split correctness and real host secret/scheduler operation demonstrated. A fixture population used in tests must never appear as live community data.

Phase 4 — read-only pilot discovery: sanitized projection, bounded map queries, distinct visual layer, details/evidence/unknown text and cache invalidation. Launch in a small area with real eligible contributors with contribution enabled and staffed moderation. Gate: privacy review, source/license review, measured budget guard, no safety/legal overclaims and kill-switch drill. Routing integration remains off initially.

Phase 5 — selective routing/offline: only eligible reviewed infrastructure with validated graph/access/direction participates; add expiring snapshots, tombstone synchronization and rollback. Gate: disconnected/stale/restricted candidates never produce a misleading route. Expand geography only after pilot metrics and moderation capacity justify it.

## Product decisions and open questions

User-selected behavior: automatic contribution ON by default, a clear off switch, no per-workout approval and no automatic public activity sharing. Proposed implementation defaults permit work to start: only eligible newly recorded activities; no silent historical backfill; no public author identities; five-owner/three-day gate; discovery before routing; no photos until their review gate; conservative pilot budgets; unknown on ambiguity. All are configurable server-side, versioned and require changes to associated fixtures/copy when altered.

Before public launch, product must name the moderation operator; approve disclosure/retention/deletion expectations and launch regions; settle age/minor eligibility; choose how landowner/legal closure evidence is assessed; set field-calibrated quality and support thresholds; choose source refresh cadence; confirm source/database licensing obligations; and validate affordable hosted ceilings. Open engineering questions include a compatible retained OSM network source, exact revision storage, moderator authentication, encryption/key lifecycle if any additional private input is retained, and offline revocation freshness expectations. Unresolved launch decisions keep publication disabled while contract/private-pipeline work proceeds.

The target is a complete, testable and retractable contribution system with explicit uncertainty. No design can promise zero errors, GPS-proven legal access, guaranteed safety, or instant deletion from a device that remains offline.
