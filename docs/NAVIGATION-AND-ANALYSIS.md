# Navigation and analysis — 10 October 2026

RIVL now has a destination-to-route screen in Routes. It geocodes a place on iOS/Android (or accepts coordinates), gets the current position, requests pedestrian or bicycle graph routing with maneuver steps, draws the route, follows live foreground GPS, speaks approaching turns, and reroutes after three accurate off-route fixes. A saved route can also be opened for guidance. Mute, repeat, stop and arrival controls are available. Speech uses the device voice at a measured rate, prefers an installed enhanced English voice, and requests a separate iOS audio session to manage mixing/ducking with other audio. The exact sound and earphone routing depend on the device and its installed voices. There is no paid speech API.

Path safety is deliberately conservative. At start, guidance states map/path coverage can be incomplete. A one-time nearby Overpass query may warn when it returns no mapped walking paths or cycle tracks/lanes in a complete 1.2 km response. This is **not** a claim that no safe facility exists, nor a route-wide lane check. Local signs and traffic take priority. Failed or limited path queries produce no absence warning. Public route/Overpass servers and tile hosts have no production-scale availability guarantee. Navigation currently runs only while its screen is open; background and lock-screen guidance are not implemented. No destination or trip history is sent to a paid provider.

Activity detail now offers a 2D route replay marker and a measured rolling power curve at 5 s, 30 s, 1 min, 5 min, 20 min and 1 h when continuous power samples exist. Sensor gaps are excluded. Athletes can enter a measured FTP in Settings; their own activities then show seven power zones using measured intervals. Tapping a pace, heart-rate, elevation or power chart marks the matching route position. Split rows compare moving time, climb and pace change against the previous full kilometre. This is not a 3D flyover or full Strava-level analysis; richer lap data and native terrain remain open.

3D terrain on native iOS/Android, verified segment ranking processing and hosted community operations, watch apps, Bluetooth sensors, offline map packs, and background turn guidance are not implemented by this change. Full-city pedestrian/cycle coverage cannot be guaranteed from OpenStreetMap or another provider. Hosted backend deployment and real-device audio/GPS checks require those environments and remain unverified.

Validation: TypeScript, 107 unit tests and development security preflight pass; iOS JavaScript bundle exports. Native ExpoSpeech pod installed. A signed native simulator build succeeded with Xcode output in system temp, which avoids Desktop file-provider metadata on copied frameworks. The signed simulator app launches. A Maestro flow passed destination entry, live route start, voice controls and stop on a simulated short walking route. Real-device audio and road conditions remain unverified.
